## Round 1

## Round 1 · 15 s demo

- review.mjs: no warnings. By eye at 540 and 1080: the head's flat front while growing (the body
  drawn from a moving s0) read as a cut box → a separate bud: the head range of a smaller worm
  whose neck sits on the cut, scaled up (`bud()`). Crossfading strip-drawn worms showed the strip
  seams as stripes → no overlap when alpha < 1 (over an opaque body the hairline gaps vanish), and
  pops instead of fades. Gut pockets read as tiger stripes → blurred and faint outside x-ray.
- Lesson: a regrowing part is its own small copy growing out of the wound, not a mask sliding open.

## Round 2 · 20 s demo: radiation, under water, less text

- The user asked for the most hooking result, then for jellyfish-like water visuals «here and
  there», then for less text. Added: a Geiger counter (needle into the red, coiled cord, probe),
  twelve wooden mannequins (12 deadly human doses: 60 Gy for the worm vs ~5 Gy for a person —
  the user's «1,000 people» from the tardigrade video does not hold for planaria), a glass needle
  putting ONE cell into an ashen worm that comes back (Wagner et al. 2011). Two shots under water
  (regrow, follow): a pebbly bed out of focus, caustics, light shafts, silt at three depths.
  Captions only on the hook, the dose and the end; the rest is told by objects (a day counter, a
  hand-written «neoblasts»).
- Seen by eye: caustics first read as a polygon net (sharp, too many, too bright) → fewer rounder
  rings, blurred, alpha 0.2; the bed too bright and flat → darker, a light pool where the worm is.
  «×» is missing in Anton → «12X». review.mjs: no warnings.
- Lesson: in a facts Short, tell with objects and keep text for the claim a picture can't carry
  (a number, a name).
